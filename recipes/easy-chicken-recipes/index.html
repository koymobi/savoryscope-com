5 Easy Chicken Recipes for a Quick Family Dinner

Five flexible ideas that keep chicken dinner interesting.

By Daniel Hart•August 3, 2026

Chicken is often called versatile when what we really mean is forgiving: it can move from a skillet to a soup pot, take on sharp marinades, or sit quietly beside vegetables. The catch is that lean breasts and richer thighs do not behave the same way. These five dinners use each cut where it makes sense, so “easy” does not have to mean dry.

Herby sheet-pan chicken thighs

Rub bone-in thighs with olive oil, dried oregano, salt, and lemon zest. Roast them skin-side up at 425°F (220°C) with wedges of red onion and small potatoes. Add quick-cooking vegetables such as asparagus only during the final twelve minutes. The thighs are ready when the skin is crisp and the thickest part reaches at least 165°F (74°C), though dark meat is often more tender a little beyond that.

Ginger chicken lettuce cups

Ground chicken cooks in under ten minutes and takes seasoning quickly. Brown it in a hot skillet, leaving it untouched long enough to gain color. Stir in grated ginger, soy sauce, and a small spoonful of brown sugar. Off the heat, add lime juice and chopped scallions. Spoon the mixture into crisp lettuce leaves with rice or sliced cucumber.

Creamy mustard cutlets

Slice chicken breasts horizontally into thin cutlets and season them before the pan heats. Sear for two or three minutes per side, then set aside. Deglaze the skillet with a little stock, whisk in Dijon mustard and crème fraîche, and return the chicken just long enough to finish cooking. The thin pieces stay tender because the sauce is made after, not around, a slowly simmering breast.

Tomato-braised chicken with olives

For a dinner that tastes longer-cooked than it is, brown boneless thighs and simmer them for twenty minutes with canned tomatoes, garlic, and a handful of olives. Keep the pan partly uncovered so the sauce reduces. Orange zest is unexpectedly good here; it gives the tomato a fragrant edge without making the dish sweet.

Chicken and corn soup

Bring good broth to a simmer with sliced mushrooms and the white parts of scallions. Add thin strips of chicken breast and cook gently until opaque. Stir in corn, then stream in a beaten egg while moving the broth in one direction. Sesame oil and black pepper at the table make the simple soup feel complete.

Two habits that prevent dry chicken

First, use a thermometer. Color is unreliable, especially with bone-in pieces, and cutting repeatedly lets juices escape. Second, match the cut to the method. Breasts like quick, attentive cooking; thighs tolerate longer heat and reward browning. In either case, rest the meat for five minutes before slicing.

These recipes are deliberately open-ended. Broccoli can replace asparagus, yogurt can stand in for crème fraîche, and chickpeas can stretch the braise. The useful recipe is the one that still works when the refrigerator is not exactly as planned.

Buying and storing chicken

If you will not cook fresh chicken within two days, freeze it while it is still at its best. Separate pieces before freezing so you can thaw only what dinner requires. The safest thaw is overnight in the refrigerator on a rimmed plate, not on the counter. For faster thawing, seal the chicken well and submerge it in cold water, changing the water every thirty minutes.

Raw chicken does not need rinsing; splashing water can spread bacteria around the sink. Pat it dry with paper towel when browning is the goal, then wash hands, board, and knife with hot soapy water. These unglamorous habits matter more than any marinade. Good weeknight cooking is partly flavor and partly making the routine easy enough to repeat safely.
